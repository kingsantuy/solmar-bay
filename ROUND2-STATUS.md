### Renderer worker, session 6534b9bc

Wrote the raw WebGL2 renderer, 34,293 bytes against a 48,000 budget: webgl.js at 5,098 bytes, gltf.js at 3,741 bytes, models.js at 505 bytes, and render.js at 24,949 bytes. No blank lines, no full-line comments, no libraries, no three.js references. The renderer provides a create-renderer entry point returning build-city, sync, screen-to-ground, project, resize, dispose, and models-ready operations. Build-city constructs static geometry once and returns a promise that resolves when model loading finishes and never rejects. Sync rebuilds every dynamic mesh from state on each call and is idempotent and complete, so the round-1 invisible-car failure mode, where a late model load cleared the cars, is structurally impossible. The camera is a chase camera with exponential lag, a snap on the first frame, trauma-squared summed-sine shake, and a field of view that widens from 62 to 68 at top speed. Golden hour is done with a warm low sun at about 17 degrees, hemisphere light, warm fog from 500 to 2400 units, a sky dome gradient with a sun disc and glow, emissive lit windows, and exposure 1.15. Shadows are contact-shadow blobs, not a shadow map; the contract allowed either, and the reliable one was picked. Trees, lamps, and containers use instanced draws. The minimal glTF loader handles the entry's own Blender models, which were inspected read-only first: all ten models carry position and normal data only, indexed triangles, flat base-color materials, no textures, images, skins, or animations. The loader handles interleaved position and normal data with a 24-byte stride, unsigned short indices, transform and matrix nodes, base-color factors and emissive factors. All ten models parsed cleanly in Node: building A with 6 primitives and 5,340 vertices, building B with 6 primitives and 1,692 vertices, the helicopter with 14 primitives and 1,003 vertices, the hotel with 11 primitives and 1,608 vertices, two palm variants, the police car, the sedan, the sports car, and the taxi. Cars are drawn at double scale with an 11-unit lift, palms at triple scale, buildings fitted to footprint and height. Each car is at most two draw calls. The renderer also exposes a project-to-screen alias, a statistics getter reporting draw calls and triangles, and a window flag set when models finish loading. The taxi uses the sedan model; the taxi model file is loaded but unused for cars. Verification was Node-only: clean dynamic imports, a numerically verified normal-matrix helper, and a loader that returns null rather than throwing on garbage or empty input. No browser verification was done by this worker, so the coordinator must confirm from screenshots that the player car is visible while driving. Two honest uncertainties remain: the facing direction of the glTF cars and helicopter was assumed to be nose along positive X, and the helicopter hover lift was set to 6 units above its height; both are only confirmable from a screenshot.
